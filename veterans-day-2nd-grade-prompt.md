# Veterans Day Escape Room Prompt (2nd Grade) - Mr. Pencil & Co

You are a Teachers Pay Teachers product designer and a 2nd-grade curriculum writer. Build me a complete, ORIGINAL, sellable Veterans Day resource for my store Mr. Pencil & Co.

WHAT I AM MAKING
A Veterans Day Reading Passage + Math Escape Room for 2nd grade (ages 7 to 8). It must be LOW PREP and work two ways:
1. Digital version, written as a Google Form with sections, so a teacher can rebuild it in under 15 minutes from my build guide.
2. Printable version, a US Letter PDF (8.5 x 11 in, 0.5 in margins) so the same activity works with no devices.

This is my own product. Do NOT copy any existing Veterans Day escape room. Write every passage, question, word problem, code, and clue from scratch. Use no trademarked characters, no borrowed images, no real service member names, no real organization names, and no text lifted from any source.

STORY FRAME (original, kid friendly)
The class joins "Operation Parade Trunk." Mr. Pencil has a locked Parade Trunk that holds the class thank-you banner for the town's Veterans Day parade. The parade starts at 11:00, and the trunk has a 4-digit lock. Each mission the students finish gives them ONE digit. When they collect all 4 digits, they unlock the trunk and "carry" the banner in the parade. Keep every sentence of the story at a 2nd-grade reading level (about Lexile 420 to 550).

THE 4 MISSIONS (in this order)

Mission 1 - Reading Passage: "Why We Have Veterans Day"
- 180 to 220 words, 3 short paragraphs with a bold heading for each. Sentences 8 to 12 words. Mostly decodable and high-frequency words, plus 4 vocabulary words that are defined inside the text: veteran, military, honor, salute.
- Facts to include, kept simple and accurate for 7-year-olds: a veteran is a person who served in the military; the military has branches (name Army, Navy, Air Force, Marines, Coast Guard, Space Force in one sentence); Veterans Day is November 11 every year; it began when a big war ended on November 11 many years ago (no war names, no battle details); Veterans Day thanks all veterans, living and past, which is different from Memorial Day; ways to celebrate: flags, parades, wearing a red poppy, saying thank you, and a quiet moment at 11:00 in the morning.
- Add a small "Word Box" under the passage with the 4 vocabulary words and a 5-to-8-word kid definition for each.
- 6 comprehension questions: 3 multiple choice (4 answer choices each, one clearly correct, distractors that are plausible but wrong), 1 true/false with "fix the false sentence" line, 1 text-evidence question ("Which sentence in the passage tells you...?" with 3 sentence choices), and 1 short answer written in one complete sentence (for the Google Form, accept any answer containing a key word you name).
- The digit for this mission is revealed after all 6 answers are correct.

Mission 2 - Math Word Problems
- 8 word problems, all Veterans Day themed (flags, poppies, thank-you cards, parade drums, medals, marching bands, banners).
- Skills for 2nd grade ONLY, one problem per skill, in this order:
  1. Two-digit addition within 100 WITH regrouping (example: 47 + 38).
  2. Two-digit subtraction within 100 WITH regrouping (example: 62 - 25).
  3. "How many more" comparison within 100.
  4. Two-step problem within 100 (add then subtract, or subtract then add).
  5. Skip counting by 5s, 10s, or 100s (rows of flags or poppies, total no higher than 500).
  6. Repeated addition with equal groups arranged in an array, no bigger than 5 rows of 5 (describe the array in words and draw it in the PDF).
  7. Money: adding coins and dollar bills to buy poppies or cards, total under $2.00, use the $ and cents signs correctly.
  8. Time: the parade starts at 11:00 a.m.; the problem asks a time to the nearest 5 minutes or an elapsed time in 5-minute jumps, no more than 30 minutes (describe the clock face in words for the form, draw it in the PDF).
- Each problem is 2 to 3 short sentences with a picture cue described in words. Every answer is a single number, money amount, or time.
- Include a "Show Your Thinking" work box under each problem in the PDF.

Mission 3 - Dog Tag Decoder
- Give a decoder key: A=1, B=2 ... Z=26.
- Students decode 4 words on 4 dog tags. Words must be 5 to 7 letters and Veterans Day themed, for example HONOR, BRAVE, SALUTE, PARADE, POPPY.
- Twist for 2nd grade: on dog tags 1 and 2, show the numbers plainly with dashes (8-15-14-15-18). On dog tags 3 and 4, show each letter as a small addition or subtraction fact within 20 that the student must solve first (for example 4+4 = 8 = H). Lay out each tag as a row of boxes: fact on top, number in the middle, letter on the bottom.
- The number of letters in the last dog tag word gives the digit for this mission.

Mission 4 - Scramble and Sentence Builder
- 6 scrambled Veterans Day words, 4 to 6 letters each (examples: flag, hero, brave, salute, honor, poppy, medal). Give a word bank with pictures described in words. One letter box in each unscrambled word is marked with a star.
- After the 6 words, students copy the 6 starred letters in order to spell a secret 6-letter word (for example THANKS). Then students write ONE complete thank-you sentence to a veteran using at least 2 words from the word bank, with a capital letter and end mark (this part is not code-checked; it is for the teacher to read).
- The number of letters in the secret word is the final digit.

Finale - Students enter the 4-digit code. If correct, show a celebration screen: "You unlocked the Parade Trunk! Thank you, veterans!" Include a "Design the Class Banner" printable page as the last activity (a wide blank banner with a short writing line under it: "Our banner says thank you because...").

CLUES WHEN A STUDENT IS WRONG
For every question in every mission, write a hint message that appears on a wrong answer. Hints must be warm and short (under 15 words), never give the answer, and point the child back to the text, the picture, or a strategy. Example tone: "Read paragraph 2 again. What is a veteran?" or "Check your regrouping. Did you carry the ten?"

GOOGLE FORM BUILD GUIDE
Write step-by-step teacher instructions to set up the form: one section per mission, response validation on every answer (number equals, text contains, or regular expression, tell them exactly which one to pick), exact text to paste for the hint (error message), how to accept both "$1.25" and "1.25" on the money problem, how to accept "11:15" and "11:15 a.m." on the time problem, and how to force a correct answer before moving to the next section. Include the exact numbers for the 4-digit code and where each digit is revealed. Assume the teacher has a Google account but is not tech savvy.
