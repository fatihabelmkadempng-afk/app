# Veterans Day Escape Room Prompt (3rd Grade) - Mr. Pencil & Co

You are a Teachers Pay Teachers product designer and a 3rd-grade curriculum writer. Build me a complete, ORIGINAL, sellable Veterans Day resource for my store Mr. Pencil & Co.

WHAT I AM MAKING
A Veterans Day Reading Passage + Math Escape Room for 3rd grade (ages 8 to 9). It must be LOW PREP and work two ways:
1. Digital version, written as a Google Form with sections, so a teacher can rebuild it in under 15 minutes from my build guide.
2. Printable version, a US Letter PDF (8.5 x 11 in, 0.5 in margins) so the same activity works with no devices.

This is my own product. Do NOT copy any existing Veterans Day escape room. Write every passage, question, word problem, code, and clue from scratch. Use no trademarked characters, no borrowed images, no real service member names, no real organization names, and no text lifted from any source.

STORY FRAME (original, kid friendly)
The class joins "Operation Honor Wall." The school's Honor Wall display case is locked, and inside are the folded flag and the class thank-you letters for the Veterans Day ceremony at 11:00 a.m. Mr. Pencil lost the code. The case has a 4-digit lock. Each mission the students finish gives them ONE digit. When they collect all 4 digits, they unlock the case and "hang" the letters before the ceremony starts. Keep every sentence of the story at a 3rd-grade reading level (about Lexile 520 to 700).

THE 4 MISSIONS (in this order)

Mission 1 - Reading Passage: "The Story Behind Veterans Day"
- 260 to 320 words, 4 paragraphs, each with a bold heading. Sentences 9 to 14 words. Include 6 vocabulary words defined in context: veteran, military, armistice, honor, ceremony, respect.
- Text features to include: a small timeline box with 3 dates (November 11, 1918: the fighting in a world war stopped at 11:00 a.m.; 1938: Armistice Day became a holiday; 1954: the name changed to Veterans Day to honor all veterans), a "Did You Know?" fact box about the red poppy, and one caption under a described image. No battle details, no casualty numbers, no war names beyond "a world war."
- Facts to include, kept accurate and simple: a veteran is a person who served in the military; the six branches (Army, Navy, Air Force, Marines, Coast Guard, Space Force); Veterans Day honors all veterans, living and past, while Memorial Day honors those who died in service; ways people celebrate: parades, ceremonies, flags, poppies, a moment of silence at 11:00 a.m., and thanking veterans in person.
- 7 comprehension questions: 2 main idea and key detail multiple choice (4 choices each), 1 vocabulary-in-context multiple choice, 1 text-feature question ("Use the timeline. In what year...?"), 1 sequence question (put 3 events in order, answer is a 3-letter code such as B-A-C), 1 inference true/false with a "prove it" line where students copy the sentence that supports the answer, and 1 constructed response of 2 to 3 sentences (for the Google Form, accept any answer containing 2 key words you name).
- The digit for this mission is revealed after all 7 answers are correct.

Mission 2 - Math Word Problems
- 8 word problems, all Veterans Day themed (flag rows, poppy bunches, thank-you letters, parade floats, medals, ceremony chairs, marching bands, banners).
- Skills for 3rd grade ONLY, one problem per skill, in this order:
  1. Multiplication within 100 with equal groups (example: 7 rows of 8 chairs).
  2. Division within 100 with equal shares (example: 56 poppies shared by 8 classes).
  3. Two-step problem using two different operations (example: multiply, then subtract).
  4. Three-digit addition within 1,000 with regrouping (example: 347 + 286).
  5. Three-digit subtraction within 1,000 with regrouping across a zero (example: 500 - 238).
  6. Rounding to the nearest 10 AND nearest 100 (one number, two answers written as one line, such as "480 and 500").
  7. Elapsed time to the minute using the 11:00 a.m. ceremony (example: the band practiced from 10:17 to 10:52; how many minutes?). Describe the clock face in words for the form, draw it in the PDF.
  8. Perimeter or area of a rectangular banner using whole-number side lengths no larger than 12 (state which one is asked and include the unit in the answer).
- Each problem is 2 to 4 sentences with a picture cue described in words. Every answer is a single number, a pair of rounded numbers, a time in minutes, or a measurement with its unit.
- Include a "Show Your Strategy" work box under each problem in the PDF and a one-line "Check" prompt (for example, "Use the opposite operation to check.").

Mission 3 - Dog Tag Decoder
- Give a decoder key: A=1, B=2 ... Z=26.
- Students decode 4 words on 4 dog tags. Words must be 6 to 8 letters and Veterans Day themed, for example SALUTE, FREEDOM, COURAGE, RESPECT, VETERAN, SERVICE.
- Twist for 3rd grade: on dog tags 1 and 2, each letter is shown as a multiplication or division fact within 100 the student solves first (for example 3 x 6 = 18 = R). On dog tags 3 and 4, use a shift cipher: give a second key where every letter moves 2 places forward (A becomes C), show the coded letters, and have students shift back. Lay out each tag as a row of boxes: fact or coded letter on top, number in the middle, letter on the bottom.
- The number of letters in the last dog tag word gives the digit for this mission.

Mission 4 - Riddle Crossword and Thank-You Letter
- 6 riddles, each 2 lines, whose answers are Veterans Day words of 5 to 8 letters (examples: honor, poppy, parade, salute, freedom, ceremony). In the PDF the answers fill a small crossword grid; in the Google Form each riddle is a short-answer question. One letter box in each answer is marked with a star.
- After the 6 riddles, students copy the 6 starred letters in order to spell a secret 6-letter word. Then students write a short thank-you letter to a veteran: greeting, 3 sentences (one reason, one detail, one wish), and a closing (this part is not code-checked; it is for the teacher to read).
- The number of letters in the secret word is the final digit.

Finale - Students enter the 4-digit code. If correct, show a celebration screen: "You unlocked the Honor Wall! Thank you, veterans!" Include a "Design a Medal of Thanks" printable page as the last activity (a large blank medal outline with a ribbon, and 2 lines under it: "This medal is for..." and "because...").

CLUES WHEN A STUDENT IS WRONG
For every question in every mission, write a hint message that appears on a wrong answer. Hints must be warm and short (under 15 words), never give the answer, and point the student back to the text, the text feature, the picture, or a strategy. Example tone: "Check the timeline box. Which year changed the name?" or "Regroup across the zero. Try it with base-ten blocks."

GOOGLE FORM BUILD GUIDE
Write step-by-step teacher instructions to set up the form: one section per mission, response validation on every answer (number equals, text contains, or regular expression, tell them exactly which one to pick), exact text to paste for the hint (error message), how to accept both "480 and 500" and "480, 500" on the rounding problem, how to accept "35" and "35 minutes" on the elapsed time problem, how to accept an answer with or without the unit on the perimeter or area problem, how to accept the sequence code with or without dashes, and how to force a correct answer before moving to the next section. Include the exact numbers for the 4-digit code and where each digit is revealed. Assume the teacher has a Google account but is not tech savvy.
